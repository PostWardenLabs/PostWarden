Journal

Every posted entry, most recent first, 50 at a time. History here is append-only: fix a mistake with Reverse on the entry itself, never by editing a posted line — the database refuses edits and deletes outright, so this isn't just a convention. + New entry (or Alt+E) opens a keyboard-first form right on this page: Tab flows account → debit/credit → memo across a line, Enter/ Shift+Enter move straight down/up the column you're already in, Alt+N adds a blank line (one also appears on its own once the last one is in use), and Distribute (Alt+D) fills in whatever amount would balance the entry on whichever line has focus. Alt shortcuts use the physical key, so they work the same on a Mac even though Option+letter would otherwise type an accented character. Filters (scenario, dates, account, payee, amount — including an "and" between two bounds, tags, search) narrow the list and the CSV export alike. A dropdown, a date, a checkbox, or adding/removing a tag refreshes the list the moment it changes; Search and Amount are free-typed text, so those need Enter (or the magnifying glass right on the Search box) to apply — refreshing on every keystroke there would just be noise. Clear filters resets all of it in one click, and stays grayed out when there's nothing to clear. Hide reversed entries and their reversals is its own checkbox for a related reason — a mistake you fixed with Reverse, and the reversal itself, both disappear together so a quick look through the ledger isn't cluttered with corrections you already made.

Scheduled entries

Recurring postings — rent, wages, subscriptions. Each occurrence lands in Staging on its due date automatically; nothing touches your real books until you approve it from there. A schedule itself is just a template plus a repeat rule (every N days/weeks/months) and the scenario its occurrences should eventually land in once approved.

Staging

A holding pen, not a scenario you ever post to directly — every entry here arrived from a schedule or a CSV import, never typed in by hand (the database enforces that, too). Check the ones you want, hit Approve entries, and each one becomes a real, independent posting in its target scenario. Once approved, the staged copy itself is never deleted or edited again, just marked approved, so nothing can be approved twice.

Before you approve one, though, it's still a draft — a schedule or an import proposed it, nobody's committed to it yet. Edit opens the same kind of grid New entry uses, prefilled with whatever's there: fix an amount, change which account a line hit, add a line, drop one, correct a typo in the memo, all before it ever becomes a real posting. Reject deletes the entry outright — for something that shouldn't have been proposed at all, not something to Approve and then immediately Reverse. Both stop being available the moment an entry is approved: from then on it's a real posting, and posted entries are never edited or deleted, only reversed.

Import

Brings entries in from a CSV — the same column layout Export CSV produces, so export → edit in a spreadsheet → re-import is a real round trip. Required columns: Entry # (groups rows into one entry — the value itself isn't kept), Date, Description, Account code, and exactly one of Debit / Credit per row. Reference, Payee, and Memo are optional; Scenario and Account name are ignored if present — every entry in a group must balance on its own. Imported entries land in Staging for review, exactly like a scheduled entry, targeting whichever scenario you pick on the upload form.

Templates

Reusable scaffolding for entries you post often. Save one here, then pick it from the "Load template" list on the Journal's + New entry panel to fill the whole form in one click. A template isn't a posting and isn't tracked once loaded — it just fills in fields, the same as if you'd typed them by hand.

Budget grid

Income/expense targets, one month at a time — no journal entries, no date, no counter-account, just how much. Budgeted is editable (type a number, tab to the next cell); Actual is what really posted to ACTUAL that month, and Variance is the difference. This only works for a scenario created as "income statement only" — see Scenarios below.

Reports & the simulated monthly close

Trial Balance and Balance Sheet default to a simulated close: Income/Expense activity shows only the current period (month-to-date on Trial Balance; fiscal-year-to-date on Balance Sheet, since a balance sheet has no Income Statement section to hold the rest), with everything before that folded into synthetic "Current/Prior Year Earnings (Unclosed)" lines under Equity — as if a real monthly close had run. It hasn't: no entries are ever posted for this, it's computed fresh on every page load. Check show true balances on either report to see the real, unmodified cumulative numbers instead. Income Statement is different from both — it's always a date range you pick, never a running balance since inception.

Variance

Compares two scenarios side by side, rolled up to a common level so a scenario entered straight against a summary account like "Bank" still lines up against a finer one that split Checking/Savings, instead of just not matching up at all.

Scenarios

The OneStream idea, in a ledger: ACTUAL and any full scenario (a forecast or what-if actually modeling dated transactions, like "what if I buy a house") are journal entries tagged with a scenario, and Variance is just a query across them. An income statement only scenario skips the ledger entirely — no dates, no counter-accounts, just income/expense amounts edited from the Budget grid. STAGING is neither of these to configure yourself — it's a fixed holding pen only Scheduled entries and Import can write to (see Staging above).

The New scenario form has two independent checkboxes — income statement only and require balanced entries — which looks like four possible combinations. In practice it's three: once a scenario is income statement only, it never takes a journal entry at all (the database refuses one regardless of what asks), so "require balanced entries" has nothing left to apply to and the checkbox itself hides on the form the moment you check the first one. Both are set once, at creation, and there's no edit screen for either afterward — the only thing you can ever change about an existing scenario is Lock/Unlock (blocks new entries or budget lines without touching what's already posted) on the Scenarios list itself. Pick carefully; "change my mind later" means creating a new scenario, not editing this one.

Accounts & levels

Summary accounts structure the chart; only postable leaves ever take a journal line. A child always inherits its parent's type (an Expense account can't have an Asset child) — the database enforces that, not just the form. Levels are just names for a depth in that hierarchy (depth 1 = every account's root, depth 2 their children, and so on) — purely labels over the same tree, useful mainly for picking a scenario's "base level" so it can post to a whole branch like "Bank" instead of every leaf underneath it.

Payees

Who the money went to or came from. Assign one per entry from the New entry screen; deactivating a payee here doesn't touch entries that already used it, and its entry count is a link straight through to them.
